Research

Current research interests.

I study how humans, language models, and deterministic software should share decisions in AI-assisted work. My applications span data science, investment research, and content creation, with an emphasis on quality, efficiency, and human oversight.

Working Paper

Decision-Rights Allocation: A Governance-Centric Perspective on LLM-Enabled Agentic Systems

A framework for assigning consequential decisions among humans, deterministic software, and language models. It interprets retrieval, validation, memory, and human review as mechanisms for allocating decision rights.

The paper proposes an analytical model for balancing adaptability, risk, auditability, and human capacity when deciding where authority should sit.

Working Paper

Decision-Trace Distillation for Reducing LLM Decision Rights in Agentic AI Systems

This paper proposes learning from recorded LLM decisions to train local components for repeated, well-defined tasks. Those components receive decision rights only when confidence, scope, and risk conditions are met; uncertain or novel cases remain with an LLM or human reviewer.

It develops the decision-trace distillation mechanism and proposes an evaluation using article structure, cover-image themes, and economic-data insights.

Working Paper

agentr: Practical Scaffolding for Coding-AI Workflows

This paper introduces agentr as a reviewable layer between human intent and coding-AI implementation. It makes task design, assumptions, and autonomy boundaries explicit before code is executed.

Its three contributions are portable task specifications, a development process combining coding assistants with human review and Git, and visual review pages for inspecting designs and approval gates.
